Frio Market Intelligence Brief: Should Frio Beverage Company enter the functional beverage category?

Question: Should Frio Beverage Company enter the functional beverage category? Prepared: 2026-08-26 | Prepared by: Frio Market Intelligence (CrewAI)

Executive Summary

No—not as a broad national entry today; Frio should stay selective and scale only through a focused functional platform.

  • Market signal: The category is large and still expanding, with US estimates of $50 billion to $66.3 billion and global growth toward $315.9 billion by 2033, but the biggest pools are concentrated in crowded sub-segments [1][2][3][4].
  • Internal signal: Frio’s functional pilot, Volt Focus, is the fastest-growing and highest-margin line in the portfolio at 39.9% YoY and 52.0% gross margin, but it is still only a 6% ACV pilot [9][10].
  • Timing & risk: The 2025 memo already chose HOLD because of cannibalization, build-vs-acquire cost, and regulatory uncertainty; external moves since then confirm the category is strategically important but increasingly expensive to enter [9][11][5][6].

The Question & Why Now

The exact decision was already addressed in the June 12, 2025 strategy memo, which recommended HOLD, continue the Volt Focus pilot, expand tracking of category M&A, and revisit with fresh market data by mid-2026 [11]. What changed is not the basic attractiveness of the category, but the level of market validation: external evidence now shows even more scale, consolidation, and investment from major beverage players, including Coca-Cola’s Simply Pop, PepsiCo’s acquisition of Poppi, and PepsiCo’s deepened partnership with Celsius [5][6][7]. That makes the category more clearly strategic, but it also raises the cost and competitive intensity of entry. Frio’s internal portfolio data still show the same asymmetry: functional is the fastest-growing and highest-margin line, yet it remains a small pilot relative to the core portfolio, and the company’s best functional adjacency, Volt Sports, is already scaled enough to create cannibalization risk [9][10][11].

Market Landscape

The functional beverage category is large and still expanding, making it a credible adjacency rather than a niche bet: US market estimates range from $50 billion to $66.3 billion, with forecast growth of 4.5% CAGR (2022–2027) to 6.2% CAGR (2024–2029), while global estimates are roughly $166 billion in 2025 and projected to reach $315.9 billion by 2033 [1][2][3][4]. Growth is being pulled by specific benefit occasions, not “function” in the abstract: hydration, energy/alertness, and protein fortification are the clearest demand drivers, with consumers seeking hydration for health, vitality, performance, and recovery and looking for ingredients like electrolytes and amino acids [1]. The category is fragmenting into distinct sub-segments, with the biggest pools still tied to legacy beverage occasions; energy and sports drinks account for more than half of all functional beverage sales [2]. There is still white space in smaller, faster-evolving functional formats, including sports protein RTD and meal replacements at $6.4 billion, functional waters at $4.1 billion, and functional RTD coffees and teas at $1.8 billion, plus protein water and proactive hydration opportunities [1]. The market is being shaped by both liquid RTDs and powder/dry formats, which broadens entry options but also raises competitive complexity [2].

Competitive Dynamics

The competitive pattern is incumbents build or buy at scale, not passive observation: Coca-Cola launched Simply Pop into prebiotic soda, while PepsiCo moved from observing to acquiring Poppi for $1.95 billion and then deepened its relationship with Celsius through a new strategic investment [5][6][7]. The most attractive competitive pockets are already showing proof of scale, which makes them harder to ignore and harder to enter cheaply: Olipop turned profitable in 2024 with sales doubling to $400 million, Poppi exceeded $500 million in sales, and Olipop raised $50 million at a $1.85 billion valuation [5][8]. The competitive field is consolidating around a handful of names that combine scale, velocity, and consumer relevance, including Abbott, Keurig Dr Pepper, Bloom Nutrition, Liquid I.V., Celsius Holdings, Olipop, Coca-Cola, PepsiCo, Danone, and Starbucks [2]. A beverage-industry visibility snapshot also cited Celsius, Liquid Death, Olipop, and Poppi as the leading brands in the functional beverage conversation in the US [12]. The move set suggests big beverage companies are prioritizing categories where function can be clearly communicated and rapidly scaled through existing channels; Simply Pop’s six grams of fiber plus vitamin C and zinc is a simple functional promise aimed directly at prebiotic soda competitors, while PepsiCo’s Poppi acquisition and Celsius partnership show a preference for acquiring or aligning with brands that already have consumer traction [5][6][7]. For a Fortune 500 beverage manufacturer, that implies the bar for entry is not category interest; it is differentiated benefit, channel fit, and a credible speed-to-scale plan [1][5][6].

Frio's Position

Frio’s internal sales file shows the functional category is the fastest-growing and highest-margin line in the file, but it is still a pilot, not a scaled business [9]. Functional (Volt Focus pilot) is growing 39.9% YoY with 52.0% gross margin, and revenue rises from $22.0M in 2024-Q3 to $39.6M in 2026-Q2 [9]. By contrast, Frio’s core CSD franchise is large but nearly flat at 1.0% YoY with 38.0% gross margin, and Juice & Nectars is declining at -3.0% YoY [9]. The brand portfolio identifies Volt Focus as the only brand already in the functional category, positioned as an energy + nootropic pilot launched 2025-Q1 in Texas c-stores, premium priced, targeted to 18-34, with only 6% ACV [10]. Volt Sports is the most important adjacency because it is already national at 85% ACV with cold-chain and c-store strength, and the 2025 memo explicitly raises cannibalization risk between a functional launch under Volt branding and Volt Sports [10][11]. Frio also has other routes to market and channel strength through Frio Cola, Frio Cola Zero, Cielo Citrus, Rio Frutas, Panhandle Punch, Agua Fria, Cypress Springs, Marfa Tea, Dobla Cafe, and Frio Soda Craft, but these are supporting assets rather than direct functional platforms [10].

Recommendation & Next Steps

Enter selectively, not broadly: keep the category in play through a focused platform strategy centered on Volt Focus and adjacent hydration/protein use cases, while deferring a full national launch until Frio resolves cannibalization, ingredient strategy, and scale economics [9][10][11]. Next steps: 1) expand Volt Focus distribution beyond the current Texas c-store pilot in controlled increments; 2) quantify cannibalization versus incremental lift against Volt Sports before any national Volt-branded extension; 3) define an ingredient strategy that avoids regulatory ambiguity around nootropics/adaptogens; 4) continue M&A monitoring, but only pursue acquisition if a target delivers a clearly differentiated benefit and channel fit at a defensible valuation [5][6][7][11].

Risks & Open Questions

The evidence does not settle whether a Volt-branded functional launch would add total portfolio revenue or mainly shift volume from Volt Sports [10][11]. It does not settle whether Frio can win shelf space fast enough to justify a build strategy, given the category’s crowded sub-segments and the 18-24 month execution risk highlighted in the memo [2][11]. It does not settle whether acquisition economics are acceptable, because external examples show high valuations and large checks for scaled brands [6][8]. It also does not settle the regulatory path for novel functional ingredients such as nootropics and adaptogens, which the memo flagged as unsettled and requiring a defined ingredient strategy before national commitment [11]. Finally, the market data support entry into function in general, but they do not prove that Frio should attack the most crowded pools—energy and sports—rather than narrower hydration, protein, or prebiotic adjacencies [1][2].
